सहायक SAHAAYAK सहकारी सेवा

Sahaayak

Interface design for a cooperative-owned services platform. Four audiences, one codebase: households booking a worker, cooperative members taking jobs, society staff who verify them, and the federation that plans training.

Money is the hero

Every screen that touches a rupee names where it goes. The split bar, not a logo, is the product's signature.

Trust is a stamp, not a checkmark

Cooperative societies verify on paper, with a seal. The badge borrows that, so verification reads as institutional rather than algorithmic.

Ledger, not dashboard

Ruled lines, tabular figures, double-rule totals. Borrowed from the membership register the society already keeps.

Why not the obvious palette

The two nearby defaults were rejected on purpose. Purple-and-orange gradients read as the private aggregator this product exists to displace. Government-portal blue reads as a form you have to fill, not a service you'd choose.

Bottle green carries the cooperative and agricultural association without tipping into eco-startup mint. Turmeric ochre is held back for two jobs only — emergency, and money moving — so it never becomes decoration.

Customer

Emergency weighting

On an emergency, proximity carries 60% because arriving fast is the service. On a scheduled booking the weights shift: proximity 35%, skill 25%, fairness 20%.

Implementation notes

The stamp

A circular double-ruled outline rotated −9°, at 92% opacity so it sits like ink on paper rather than a UI chip. Three sizes: 52px inline, 74px on a worker card, 104px on a profile. The pending state uses a dashed border and 40% opacity — an unstamped register entry.

The split bar

Widths are proportional but the two smallest segments are given a 1px minimum so they never disappear. Amounts print inside a segment only above 8% width; below that they move to the legend. Colours are fixed per destination and never reused elsewhere in the interface.

Two type scales

Customer and admin screens use a 13/15/19px scale. Worker screens step up to 15/17/20px with heavier weights and higher contrast — they are read outdoors, in daylight, often quickly. Same components, different scale token.

Hindi first

Every screen here is drawn in Hindi because that is the default for both customer and worker. Devanagari runs 20–30% longer than Latin, so buttons wrap to two lines rather than truncating, and no fixed-width container holds translated text.

Motion

One animation in the entire product: the search radar while matching, because a wait needs to show the system is working. Everything else responds only to a tap. No card hover lifts, no scroll reveals.

Emergency ochre

Ochre appears in exactly three places: the emergency entry point, the countdown, and the welfare fund. It is never used for a general accent, so when a user sees it they know something is either urgent or about their money.
